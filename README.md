# Renal-Failure-Project

PRF Preprocessing Pipeline: Methods

Overview	
The analytic dataset was constructed from an initial cohort of 3,692 patients and 1,302 candidate variables drawn from the Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) registry, supplemented with 418,795 intraoperative and postoperative blood pressure (BP) readings from 14 fields. Following cohort exclusions, missing-data handling, and feature engineering, the finalized preoperative dataset comprised 2,701 patients across 61 variables, with an overall postoperative renal failure (PRF) prevalence of 7.59%. Seven primary analysis cases were subsequently constructed by combining preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative feature sets, along with one supplementary case restricted to intraoperative mean arterial pressure (MAP) features.

Data Loading and Initial Preparation
Two source datasets were loaded: an STS clinical dataset (3,692 rows, 1,302 columns, of which 22 columns were cast to numeric type) and a BP dataset (418,795 rows, 14 columns). Body mass index (BMI) was missing for 180 patients and was imputed using recorded height and weight, resulting in complete BMI data. Of the 1,302 candidate STS variables, 73 were retained for downstream processing based on clinical relevance and completeness.

A composite race variable was derived from the multi-select source columns. Patients with “Not Documented” ethnicity were excluded (3,692 to 3,653 patients, a reduction of 39), followed by exclusion of patients with “Unknown” or “Not Documented” race (3,653 to 3,404 patients, a reduction of 249). Two operational flags were assessed at this stage: a flag for missing BP data prior to July 1, 2021, which affected 0 patients, and a flag for emergent salvage status, which identified 6 patients who were retained at this stage per analyst decision rather than excluded outright.

PRF Outcome Construction and Cohort Exclusions
The primary outcome, postoperative renal failure (PRF), was coded as present in 281 patients and absent in 3,117 patients; 6 patients were classified as indeterminate and subsequently excluded from the analytic cohort. Sequential cohort exclusion criteria were then applied. Patients on dialysis preoperatively were excluded (3,398 to 3,271 patients, –127). Patients admitted with a ventricular assist device (VAD) were evaluated for exclusion but none met criteria (3,271 to 3,271 patients, –0), while patients who received a VAD during the index hospitalization were excluded (3,271 to 3,246 patients, –25). Patients undergoing emergent salvage surgery were excluded (3,246 to 3,243 patients, –3). In parallel with these exclusions, “Unknown” responses were recoded to missing across all binary yes/no variables, and 2,411 instances of “Not documented” or “Not Documented” NYHA classification were similarly recoded to missing.
Cohort eligibility was further restricted according to procedure type. Coronary artery bypass grafting (CAB) status was recorded as No, Yes-planned, Yes-unplanned due to surgical complication, or Yes-unplanned due to unsuspected disease or anatomy, while valve surgery status was recorded as Yes or No. Only patients undergoing CABG (planned or unplanned) or valve surgery were retained, which reduced the cohort from 3,243 to 3,109 patients (–134). An additional 5 patients with baseline creatinine of 4 mg/dL or greater were excluded (3,109 to 3,104 patients). Later in preprocessing, patients with an unknown chronic lung disease (CLD) severity and type combination (see below) were also excluded, reducing the cohort from 2,961 to 2,701 patients (–260).

Column and Row Cleaning
Two variables exceeding 50% missingness were dropped from the dataset: chronic lung disease type (62.2% missing) and NYHA functional classification (73.7% missing). For variables with low overall missingness, complete-case row deletion was used rather than imputation. This included 12 numeric variables (total albumin, last creatinine level, total bilirubin, ejection fraction, white blood cell count, hemoglobin, hematocrit, platelet count, sodium, cardiopulmonary bypass time, lowest intraoperative hemoglobin, and highest intraoperative glucose) and 16 categorical variables (diabetes, hypertension, immunocompromised status, mediastinal radiation history, cancer within 5 years, peripheral artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, prior myocardial infarction, heart failure, resuscitation, inhaled medication or oral bronchodilator use, sleep apnea, liver disease, liver cirrhosis, syncope, and inotrope use within 48 hours). This row deletion step reduced the cohort from 3,104 to 2,961 patients, a loss of 143 patients. Three additional variables with intermediate missingness were noted for later handling rather than immediate deletion: INR (1.29% missing), MELD score (1.87% missing), and last A1C level (8.28% missing).

Deduplication was performed by retaining the most recent surgical encounter for each unique patient identifier (New_MRN); no duplicate encounters were identified at this stage, and the cohort size remained unchanged at 2,961 patients. Categorical variables were additionally screened for near-zero variance using the prevalence of “Yes” responses. Prevalence ranged widely across the screened variables, from 93.11% for hypertension to 0.07% for unresponsive state at presentation. Two variables were dropped for near-zero variance: Hispanic, Latino, or Spanish ethnicity (0.84% Yes) and unresponsive state (0.07% Yes); all other screened variables, including diabetes (45.53%), endocarditis (6.72%), immunocompromised status (5.07%), mediastinal radiation (1.42%), cancer within 5 years (5.57%), peripheral artery disease (13.14%), cerebrovascular disease (29.31%), prior cardiac interventions (33.84%), prior myocardial infarction (43.26%), heart failure (42.28%), cardiac arrhythmia (22.02%), inhaled medication or bronchodilator use (22.05%), sleep apnea (23.88%), liver disease (7.90%), liver cirrhosis (1.96%), syncope (2.97%), ADP inhibitor use within 5 days (3.68%), anticoagulant use within 48 hours (41.54%), inotrope use within 48 hours (4.66%), steroid use within 24 hours (1.72%), and valve surgery (39.89%), were retained.

Comorbidity and Laboratory Feature Engineering
A combined chronic lung disease variable (CLD_Combined) was created by integrating the separate severity and type fields, and the two source columns were then removed. Patients with an unknown value on both the severity and type components of this combined variable were excluded, reducing the cohort from 2,961 to 2,701 patients (–260). Hematocrit was removed because of its redundancy with hemoglobin. Last A1C level was dropped despite falling under the 50% missingness threshold, given its 7.4% missingness combined with limited independent contribution, and MELD score was dropped as a derived composite that was considered redundant with its individual laboratory components. Platelet count was retained in its original form, and a log-transformed feature, log[platelet count], was added as a derived variable. Following these steps, the preoperative dataset was finalized at 2,701 patients across 61 variables, with a PRF prevalence of 7.59%.

Blood Pressure Preprocessing
The BP dataset was aligned with the finalized clinical cohort through a series of preprocessing steps. BP readings lacking a patient identifier were first removed, reducing the dataset from 418,795 to 337,488 rows (–81,307). The remaining readings were then restricted to patients represented in the STS cohort, further reducing the dataset to 259,051 rows (–78,437) representing 2,031 unique patients. BP measurement phase (intraoperative versus postoperative) was derived directly from the bp_id field, an approach confirmed as authoritative by the clinical collaborator; observed bp_id values included arterial systolic/diastolic BP, BMDI-derived MAP, and anesthesia-recorded BP. Readings were aligned to each patient’s most recent surgical encounter, which did not change the row count (259,051 rows), and postoperative readings with a missing systolic BP value were removed, reducing the dataset to 240,220 rows (–18,831).

Before feature computation, the first three readings for each patient within each measurement phase were excluded to minimize the influence of transient perioperative artifacts. Before this exclusion, the intraoperative phase contained 133,453 readings across 1,934 patients and the postoperative phase contained 106,767 readings across 2,022 patients; after exclusion, 127,651 intraoperative readings remained across 1,933 patients and 100,701 postoperative readings remained across 2,022 patients. Patients with three or fewer readings in a given phase consequently had zero readings for that phase following this step, and were correctly coded as not having usable data for that phase. Following cleaning, 52 intraoperative and 52 postoperative BP-derived features were computed. Of the 2,701 patients in the finalized preoperative cohort, 1,933 had usable intraoperative BP data and 2,022 had usable postoperative BP data.

Feature Sets
The finalized preoperative feature set comprised 47 variables spanning demographics, comorbidities, laboratory values, medications, and clinical status: patient age, sex, BMI, diabetes, hypertension, endocarditis, tobacco use, immunocompromised status, mediastinal radiation history, cancer within 5 years, peripheral artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, total albumin, last creatinine level, total bilirubin, prior cardiac interventions, prior myocardial infarction, heart failure, cardiogenic shock, resuscitation, cardiac arrhythmia, ejection fraction, surgical status, incidence, white blood cell count, hemoglobin, platelet count (raw and log-transformed), sodium, INR, home oxygen use, inhaled medication or bronchodilator use, sleep apnea, pneumonia history, alcohol use, liver disease, liver cirrhosis, syncope, ACE inhibitor or ARB use within 48 hours, beta blocker use within 24 hours, ADP inhibitor use within 5 days, aspirin use within 5 days, anticoagulant use within 48 hours, inotrope use within 48 hours, steroid use within 24 hours, race, and the combined CLD variable. A separate set of 5 procedural variables—CABG status, valve surgery status, cardiopulmonary bypass time, lowest intraoperative hemoglobin, and highest intraoperative glucose—was defined for cases incorporating intraoperative procedural detail. As described above, 52 intraoperative and 52 postoperative BP-derived features were also available. A focused subset of 26 intraoperative MAP-specific features (central tendency, variability, extremes, rate-of-change, and threshold-based metrics such as percentage of time below 55, 65, and 75 mmHg, or above 95, 105, and 115 mmHg, and percentage of readings reflecting drops or spikes of 20, 40, or 60 mmHg) was defined for a supplementary MAP-only analysis case.

Construction of Analysis Cases
Seven primary analysis cases were defined by combining the preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative feature sets in different configurations, along with one supplementary case restricted to intraoperative MAP features. The preoperative-only case included all 2,701 patients with 47 feature columns (PRF 7.59%). The intraoperative-only case included 1,933 patients with 57 feature columns (PRF 7.35%), and the postoperative-only case included 2,022 patients with 52 feature columns (PRF 7.32%). Combined cases included preoperative plus intraoperative features (1,933 patients, 104 columns, PRF 7.35%), preoperative plus postoperative features (2,022 patients, 99 columns, PRF 7.32%), intraoperative plus postoperative features (1,924 patients, 109 columns, PRF 7.28%), and the full combination of preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative features (1,924 patients, 156 columns, PRF 7.28%). The supplementary preoperative-plus-intraoperative-MAP-only case included 1,933 patients with 73 feature columns (PRF 7.35%).

Train-Test Splitting
Each analysis case was partitioned into training and test subsets using a stratified 70/30 split based on the PRF outcome. For the preoperative-only case, this yielded a training set of 1,891 patients (PRF 8.09%) and a test set of 810 patients (PRF 6.42%). The intraoperative-only case yielded a training set of 1,354 patients (PRF 7.16%) and a test set of 579 patients (PRF 7.77%), and the postoperative-only case yielded a training set of 1,416 patients (PRF 7.56%) and a test set of 606 patients (PRF 6.77%). The preoperative-plus-intraoperative case yielded a training set of 1,354 patients (PRF 7.46%) and a test set of 579 patients (PRF 7.08%). The preoperative-plus-postoperative case yielded a training set of 1,416 patients (PRF 6.85%) and a test set of 606 patients (PRF 8.42%). The intraoperative-plus-postoperative case yielded a training set of 1,347 patients (PRF 7.57%) and a test set of 577 patients (PRF 6.59%). The fully combined case yielded a training set of 1,347 patients (PRF 7.94%) and a test set of 577 patients (PRF 5.72%). The supplementary MAP-only case yielded a training set of 1,354 patients (PRF 7.02%) and a test set of 579 patients (PRF 8.12%).

Class Balancing, Normalization, and Encoding
Within each training set, class imbalance was addressed using random undersampling (RUS) of the majority class followed by Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique for Nominal and Continuous data (SMOTE-NC) oversampling of the minority class. The target after resampling was an approximately 70:30 negative-to-positive ratio. First, random undersampling was applied to reduce the negative-to-positive ratio to approximately 4.7:1. In the preoperative-only training set, this yielded 153 positive and 714 negative cases (867 total). Numeric features (11 columns in this case) were then standardized using z-score normalization. Parameters were estimated exclusively from the training set and subsequently applied to the test set. In this case, INR required median imputation before application of SMOTE-NC. SMOTE-NC was then applied at a 2x oversampling ratio for the minority class, increasing the training set from 867 to 1,020 rows and the minority class from 153 to 306 cases, yielding a post-resampling PRF prevalence of 30.00%. Categorical variables were dummy-encoded using k–1 encoding, resulting in a final feature count of 70 and a final training set of 1,020 rows by 71 columns; the corresponding test set retained 810 rows by 71 columns, with an unresampled test-set PRF prevalence of 6.42% (52 of 810 patients).

The same sequence of undersampling, normalization, imputation, SMOTE-NC oversampling, and dummy encoding was applied independently to each remaining analysis case, with parameters determined by the feature composition of each case. For the intraoperative-only case, undersampling yielded 97 positive and 453 negative cases (550 total); 55 numeric columns were normalized with no imputation required; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 550 to 647 rows (minority class 97 to 194, PRF 29.98%); and encoding produced 57 features, for a final training set of 647 rows by 58 columns and a test set of 579 rows by 58 columns (test PRF 7.77%, 45 of 579 patients). For the postoperative-only case, undersampling yielded 107 positive and 499 negative cases (606 total); 52 numeric columns were normalized, with median imputation required for two postoperative BP rate-of-change variables; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 606 to 713 rows (minority class 107 to 214, PRF 30.01%); and encoding produced 52 features, for a final training set of 713 rows by 53 columns and a test set of 606 rows by 53 columns (test PRF 6.77%, 41 of 606 patients).

For the combined preoperative-plus-intraoperative case, undersampling yielded 101 positive and 471 negative cases (572 total); 66 numeric columns were normalized, with median imputation required for INR; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 572 to 673 rows (minority class 101 to 202, PRF 30.01%); and encoding produced 125 features, for a final training set of 673 rows by 126 columns and a test set of 579 rows by 126 columns (test PRF 7.08%, 41 of 579 patients). For the combined preoperative-plus-postoperative case, undersampling yielded 97 positive and 453 negative cases (550 total); 63 numeric columns were normalized, with median imputation required for INR and the two postoperative BP rate-of-change variables; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 550 to 647 rows (minority class 97 to 194, PRF 29.98%); and encoding produced 118 features, for a final training set of 647 rows by 119 columns and a test set of 606 rows by 119 columns (test PRF 8.42%, 51 of 606 patients).

For the combined intraoperative-plus-postoperative case, undersampling yielded 102 positive and 476 negative cases (578 total); 107 numeric columns were normalized, with median imputation required for the two postoperative BP rate-of-change variables; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 578 to 680 rows (minority class 102 to 204, PRF 30.00%); and encoding produced 110 features, for a final training set of 680 rows by 111 columns and a test set of 577 rows by 111 columns (test PRF 6.59%, 38 of 577 patients). For the fully combined preoperative-plus-intraoperative-plus-postoperative case, undersampling yielded 107 positive and 499 negative cases (606 total); 118 numeric columns were normalized, with median imputation required for INR and the two postoperative BP rate-of-change variables; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 606 to 713 rows (minority class 107 to 214, PRF 30.01%); and encoding produced 177 features, for a final training set of 713 rows by 178 columns and a test set of 577 rows by 178 columns (test PRF 5.72%, 33 of 577 patients).
For the supplementary preoperative-plus-intraoperative-MAP-only case, undersampling yielded 95 positive and 443 negative cases (538 total); 37 numeric columns were normalized, with median imputation required for INR; SMOTE-NC increased the training set from 538 to 633 rows (minority class 95 to 190, PRF 30.02%); and encoding produced 94 features, for a final training set of 633 rows by 95 columns and a test set of 579 rows by 95 columns (test PRF 8.12%, 47 of 579 patients).
